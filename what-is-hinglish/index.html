What is Hinglish?

A complete guide to the Hindi-English mix that dominates how India communicates every day.

The short answer

Hinglish is the everyday combination of Hindi words, English words and the Roman alphabet. Instead of writing pure Hindi in Devanagari script or pure English, millions of Indians blend both languages in the same sentence — using a standard English keyboard. Think of phrases like "Mujhe call karo later" or "The meeting was bahut boring." It is not a formal language, but it is the dominant communication style in Indian texting, social media and casual conversation.

Where does the word Hinglish come from?

The term "Hinglish" is a portmanteau of "Hindi" and "English." It first appeared in Indian media in the late 1990s as television and advertising began mixing both languages openly. Before the word existed, the practice itself had already been around for decades — Indian families, friends and coworkers had been switching between Hindi and English for generations. The label simply gave a name to something people were already doing naturally.

Is Hinglish a real language?

Linguists generally classify Hinglish as code-switching — the practice of alternating between two languages within a conversation or even within a single sentence. It does not have its own grammar rules, formal vocabulary or standardized spelling. However, in practical terms, Hinglish is absolutely real. Hundreds of millions of people use it daily, it dominates Indian digital communication, and many Hinglish words have entered formal dictionaries. Whether it counts as a "language" or a "communication style" depends on how you define the word.

How did Hinglish become so popular?

Bollywood played a huge role — Hindi films have always mixed English dialogue into Hindi scripts, and audiences loved it. The rise of mobile phones and texting in the early 2000s accelerated the trend because typing in Devanagari was difficult on early keypads while English letters were easy. Social media platforms like Facebook, WhatsApp, Twitter and Instagram then cemented Hinglish as the default online language for Indian youth. Today, advertising agencies, news outlets and even government campaigns use Hinglish to reach wider audiences.

How is Hinglish written?

Hinglish is written using the Roman (English) alphabet. There is no strict spelling standard — different people spell the same Hindi word differently depending on how it sounds to them. For example, "मैं" can be written as "main," "mai," or even "mein." As long as the pronunciation is clear, Hinglish readers understand it. This flexibility is one reason Hinglish spread so quickly: anyone with an English keyboard can write it without learning a new script.

Who speaks Hinglish?

Reports commonly cite that roughly 42% of Indians speak Hindi or its variants, and a massive portion of that daily usage is actually Hinglish. In metro cities like Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore and Hyderabad, Hinglish is often the default language for casual conversation, even among people whose mother tongue is neither Hindi nor English. It cuts across age groups, education levels and regions, making it arguably the most widely understood informal language style in India.
